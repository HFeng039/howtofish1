# How to Fish guide

**The cleanest How to Fish guide route is to finish the five-island story first: Lighthouse → Forest → Desert → Rocks → Volcano.** Complete each NPC hand-in, buy the upgrade that removes your current blocker, and save broad achievement cleanup for after the ending. The collected evidence consistently describes this as less wasteful than chasing optional content during the story.

This page filters unrelated “how to fish” cooking, Minecraft, Palworld, Stardew Valley, and real-life fishing results out of the research set. It focuses on Dazed Games’ Steam game *How to Fish*. Facts below reflect the collected evidence checked on August 28, 2026; anything not confirmed there is marked **to be confirmed**.

## What kind of game is this?

*How to Fish* is a physics-based fishing action game with combat, island progression, trick shots, cooking, gambling, and online co-op. You catch creatures, kill or process them, sell the results, and buy rods, lures, weapons, boats, and inventory upgrades. The loop sounds simple, but islands introduce new bait tiers, shops, bosses, and traversal objectives.

The important mindset is that fishing is rarely the whole objective. A catch can be money, food, a quest item, boss bait, a collection entry, or a gambling stake. Before you sell or cook something unusual, ask whether a hand-in or later boss needs it.

## Follow the five-island order

The route begins at the Lighthouse, then moves through the Forest, Desert, Rocks, and military/Volcano area. Evidence describes five main island stops and uses NPC hand-ins plus Radar coordinates to confirm when a stage is complete. Don’t sail toward a new dot until the current island’s objective and next destination have actually been unlocked.

On each island, the broad pattern is the same: find the NPC’s request, obtain the required bait, summon or fight the boss, return the trophy, and buy the upgrade that makes the next trip safer. Optional shops and systems can help, but they should not replace the current progression goal.

## Lighthouse and Forest

The opening island teaches crab fishing, melee combat, and the first boss chain. Evidence describes buying a crab-fishing rod, selling or feeding catches, upgrading early melee gear, and using a Beer hand-in to receive the Empty Beer Can needed for the Spider Crab. Defeat it, return its shell, take the boat keys, buy the Radar, and sail to the next island.

The Forest introduces firearms, Beginner lures, Dynamite, and trick shots. The main hand-in chain uses three Leeches to obtain the Giant Piranha bait. Kill the Giant Piranha, return its skeleton, update the Radar, and buy the boat upgrade before chasing the yellow destination dot.

## Desert, Rocks, and Volcano

The Desert island adds Standard lures, an SMG, more weapon attachments, cooking, and the vacation quest line. This is also where evidence mentions the Grillmaster and Blue Shark as an optional chain, while the story’s Tourist, endangered creature, Carrot, and Pufferfish chain continues progression. Buy the Boat Radar upgrade before leaving.

The Rocks island adds Professional lures, a Sniper Rifle, a casino, and the Professional Boss Lure route. Evidence describes catching a Tuna, using it to summon the Albatross, defeating it, and delivering its head to unlock the final island. Keep the trophy safe until the NPC accepts it.

The Volcano is the military endgame. Evidence describes five fish for the Scientist, the Fish Bucket bait, the Bowhead Whale, and the Mutated Bowhead Whale that appears after the whale is thrown into the volcano. After the final hand-in, evidence says you take the black boat back to the mainland and can continue the same save after credits.

## Build the economy around progression

Early money comes from catching and selling, not from fighting everything at once. Use the best lure tier available for your current island, because older lures eventually target weaker pools. Inventory space matters too; evidence repeatedly points to Extra Inventory Slot purchases so you can carry quest items, healing food, and profitable catches at the same time.

Hunger is also part of survival. Evidence describes eating to keep hunger high as a reserve health layer, rather than waiting until damage has already started. Cook when possible, carry healing creatures when inventory permits, and avoid spending money on a new weapon while an old one can still handle the current boss.

## Do story first, cleanup second

The strongest guide recommendation in the evidence is to finish story systems before serious achievement cleanup. The first pass unlocks islands, lure tiers, shops, engines, attachments, and bosses. The second pass returns with better weapons and inventory space. The third pass handles collections and dedicated challenge saves.

This reduces backtracking and prevents irreversible mistakes with quest bait. For example, evidence warns that some bosses require unusual bait such as an Empty Beer Can, Carrot, Tuna, or a whole Bowhead Whale. Selling the wrong creature may cost you a trip, while post-game cleanup lets you buy or catch replacements under better conditions.

## Post-game and achievements

The collected guide evidence lists 28 achievements. It separates story achievements from Killscore tricks, cooking, casino, collection, boat, and final-boss challenges. After the ending, you can target missing achievements with better gear and full island access instead of interrupting early progression.

For collections, check the in-game encyclopedia and treat normal creatures and Drip variants as separate goals. Evidence describes Collector as every normal creature and Fishipedia as every Drip variant. Bosses and quest creatures still need their triggers, while ordinary catches depend more on the equipped lure pool.

## Avoid these route mistakes

Don’t leave an island before the hand-in and next coordinates are confirmed. Don’t spend all your money on weapons when bait, inventory, or boat movement is the real blocker. Don’t sell unusual catches immediately; check whether an NPC or boss chain needs them.

Also don’t assume an old shortcut still works. Evidence says Patch 1.0.5 fixed a bug that allowed players to reach locked islands early, so a current route should respect normal progression. If a patch changes an objective after this page’s check date, treat the official announcement as the newer source.
