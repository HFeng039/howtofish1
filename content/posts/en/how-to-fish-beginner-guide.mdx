# How to Fish beginner guide

**For a How to Fish beginner guide, start with crabs, sell or eat deliberately, buy the Crab Fishing Rod and useful melee upgrades, and do not leave the Lighthouse until the Spider Crab chain gives you the boat.** Your first goal is not to catch everything; it is to learn the loop of catch → sell/eat → upgrade → hand-in → travel.

This page filters unrelated real-fishing, cooking, Minecraft, Palworld, and Stardew Valley search results from the research set. It uses only collected *How to Fish* evidence checked on August 28, 2026. Where the evidence does not prove a number or method, it says **to be confirmed**.

## What you actually do first

The opening puts you near the Lighthouse after a boat crash. Evidence describes picking up free clams, feeding an NPC, taking the fishing rod, and catching crustaceans. Catch, kill, and inspect a creature, then sell or eat it depending on your money and hunger.

The first purchases are about unlocking progression. A basic rod lets you fish, Hot Dog bait improves catches, a knife improves melee killing, and Beer starts the Spider Crab quest. Evidence does not require buying everything at once, so buy what removes your current bottleneck.

## Manage hunger before it becomes damage

Treat hunger as a reserve health bar. Evidence describes eating regularly so the hunger bar can heal you over time; if it empties, you start taking damage. That means food is not just inventory clutter—it is part of your survival kit.

Free clams are useful early, but don’t ignore cooked or caught food once more options unlock. If a boss is approaching, keep a healing item available instead of spending every slot on bait or weapons. A small food reserve can prevent a long walk back from a death.

## Make early money without overfarming

Sell catches when you need money for a known purchase. Evidence describes using crab catches to pay for early items and upgrades. If you already have enough for the next progression item, move on instead of farming the same pool for uncertain future needs.

Use better bait when it becomes available. The Lighthouse evidence lists Hot Dog bait as an upgrade over the free lure for more valuable creatures. Later islands continue the same principle: match the lure tier to the area, then use inventory space for profitable catches.

## Buy the right first upgrades

Evidence identifies the Crab Fishing Rod, Hot Dog bait, Brass Knuckles, Knife, Radio, Beer, and Radar at the Lighthouse shops. You do not need every item immediately. A rod creates income, a knife helps kills, and Beer is required for the first boss hand-in.

Melee upgrades matter before firearms exist. The evidence describes buying and upgrading a knife, and also mentions sharpening melee gear near the Lighthouse. If early crustaceans take too long to kill, that is usually a better first investment than another consumable.

## Complete the first boss properly

The first major beginner wall is the Spider Crab. Evidence says to buy Beer, feed it to the Lighthouse Keeper, receive the Empty Beer Can, equip the can as bait, and catch the Spider Crab. This is a chain, so don’t sell the Beer before the hand-in.

During the fight, patience is safer than panic. Evidence mentions dodging the charge and attacking during the stun. Exact health and damage numbers are **to be confirmed**; focus on not getting caught, attacking during safe windows, and retreating to reset if necessary.

After the kill, return the shell to the Lighthouse Keeper. The reward is the boat keys. Evidence then points to buying the Radar for $10 and taking the wooden motorboat, which turns navigation into your next priority.

## Prepare for the Forest island

The Forest island introduces firearms, Beginner lures, Dynamite, and trick shots. Evidence recommends buying a Fishing Rod for proper fish, using a Shotgun as a strong early firearm, collecting three Leeches, and exchanging them for Giant Piranha bait.

Keep hunger and inventory in mind before the fight. The Extra Inventory Slot purchases become more valuable because you need room for healing creatures, bait, and trophies. Evidence says Island 2’s hand-in returns the Piranha Skeleton to the woman and updates the Radar to the yellow dot.

## Beginner mistakes to avoid

The biggest mistake is selling a quest item. Beer becomes an Empty Beer Can; Leeches become bait; some later bosses use a creature as bait. If an item or catch looks unusual, check the active hand-in before selling it.

The second mistake is buying weapons while ignoring hunger, inventory, or movement. A stronger gun does not help if you die because you had no food, lose a trophy because inventory is full, or waste time because the boat lacks the next upgrade. Balance the loadout.

The third mistake is chasing optional content too early. The evidence recommends finishing the story first, then returning after the ending for achievements, collections, gambling, and trick-shot cleanup. You will have better weapons and more island access later.

## Difficulty, co-op, and saves

The game is playable solo and in online co-op. Later evidence confirms official lobby support grew to up to eight players, but beginner progression is fully understandable alone. Co-op can make some fights easier, yet it also adds coordination time.

Evidence notes Patch 1.0.9 added Easy difficulty. If early bosses are stopping progress, that is a safer option than an unofficial cheat tool. Save behavior details are discussed on the platform pages; before closing, complete hand-ins and use the in-game save flow.
