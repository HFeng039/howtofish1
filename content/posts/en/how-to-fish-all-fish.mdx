# How to Fish all fish

**For “How to Fish all fish,” treat two collections separately: Collector covers every normal creature, while Fishipedia covers the Drip variant of every creature.** The collected evidence describes a 49-creature guide target, 28 achievements, lure-based pools, and post-game cleanup as the efficient order.

This page filters out real aquariums, cooking, Minecraft, Palworld, and Stardew Valley results. It is for the Steam game *How to Fish*, using collected evidence checked on August 28, 2026. Exact per-creature spawn weights are **to be confirmed**.

## Understand the two checklists

Collector and Fishipedia are not the same list. Evidence says Collector requires every normal creature and Fishipedia requires the Drip variant of every creature. Completing a normal entry does not complete its Drip entry.

The evidence references an “All 49 Creatures” guide, so use 49 as the research target rather than inventing a new number. If your in-game encyclopedia shows a different count after a patch, treat the live game and official patch notes as newer than this research snapshot.

Fishipedia is therefore the longer grind. Drip creatures are visually obvious, have rainbow-style naming, and continue to respawn. Evidence says spending a Drip creature at the Reel of Fortune does not erase the encyclopedia record, so collection progress is not undone by gambling.

## Finish the story before collection grinding

The best order is story first. The route unlocks all five islands, lure tiers, shops, attachments, bosses, and post-game systems. Trying to complete every entry early means revisiting areas later anyway.

After the ending, you can fish from a convenient late-game spot for many ordinary entries. Evidence says the equipped lure determines the available creature pool, so you do not need to return to a particular island for every normal fish.

Bosses and quest-triggered creatures are the exception. They still need the corresponding lure or progression trigger. Keep the required Boss Lure or trophy condition until the encyclopedia entry is actually filled.

## Use lure pools deliberately

Evidence gives a broad bait ladder: free/Hot Dog at Lighthouse, Free/Beginner at Forest, Standard at Desert, Professional at Rocks, and Scientific at Volcano. The useful collection principle is that lure tier controls which ordinary pool you can draw from.

Instead of wandering, choose the pool tied to missing entries. Open the encyclopedia with Tab, identify the gaps, equip the matching lure, and fish only until that pool is no longer useful. This is less wasteful than fishing randomly with the strongest lure.

For bosses, use the correct specialized bait. Evidence lists examples such as the Empty Beer Can for Spider Crab, Modified Leech for Giant Piranha, Carrot for Pufferfish, Professional Boss Lure for Tuna, and the Bowhead Whale for the final fight. Exact every-creature bait mapping is **to be confirmed** outside the sources.

## Cycle catches without wasting casts

For missing Drip variants, evidence describes a time-saving method: cast far, watch the creature notification while reeling, and immediately cancel or release if the result is not Drip. With clean timing, one cast can cycle through several candidates.

Slow down as soon as a Drip name appears. Habitual cancelling can release the exact variant you need. Confirm the catch before returning to fast cycling.

Be careful with aggressive creatures. Evidence specifically notes that releasing a Tiger Fish does not always remove the threat; it may leave the water and attack while you focus on the next catch. Keep healing and an escape route available.

## Clean up in a controlled order

Start by marking every empty normal entry. Group them by lure pool, then by bosses and quest creatures. This produces a short list of actual blockers instead of a vague “catch everything” goal.

Next, separate Drip variants by the same lure pools. Because Drip creatures respawn, this is a rarity grind rather than a permanently missable collection. Use the cycle method only after you know the pool is correct.

Finally, make dedicated attempts for risky challenge achievements. Evidence suggests separate saves for goals like Bean and treats Handyman as a final-boss condition. Mixing a timed or one-condition run into collection cleanup increases the chance of losing both.

## Achievement context

There are 28 achievements in the collected evidence. Collection-related entries include Collector and Fishipedia. Other achievements may come naturally during the story, including first creature, early Drip creature, story hand-ins, boat upgrades, cooking, and casino tasks.

That does not mean every achievement should be attempted immediately. Evidence recommends finishing the story first, doing controlled cleanup second, and saving collection/challenge passes for last. This order reduces wasted bait and repeated travel.

## Common collection mistakes

Don’t assume a completed normal entry also counts for its Drip variant. Don’t sell every unusual creature immediately; some are bait or trophies. Don’t grind the wrong lure because it is more expensive—cost does not equal coverage.

Don’t treat the 49-creature research target as a substitute for the live encyclopedia. Use it as a planning number and confirm missing entries in-game. Patch changes after August 28, 2026 are **to be confirmed**.

Don’t ignore safety during fast cycling. Aggressive or large creatures can interrupt the loop, and a death can cost more time than the casts saved.

## Keep an audit trail while collecting

A collection page is only useful if it reflects your own save. After each session, write down missing normal entries, missing Drip entries, lure equipped, island used, and any boss still locked. This turns hundreds of casts into a short, repeatable task list.

Photograph or copy the encyclopedia only as a temporary note. The in-game Tab journal remains the authority. If an update changes a creature, bait, or collection rule, your screenshot can mislead you even though the live encyclopedia will not.

Mark whether a missing entry is ordinary, Drip, boss-triggered, or story-gated. That distinction determines whether you need a lure, a Boss Lure, a quest item, or simply more attempts. It also tells you when to stop wasting bait on the wrong pool.
